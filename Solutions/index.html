01
Data management approaches

File-based vs DBMS

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

(a) Approach and problems

The branches use a file-based approach: each branch maintains separate spreadsheet files rather than one integrated database managed by a DBMS.

  • Data isolation: stock, sales and supplier information is split across files, so head office cannot easily query them together.
  • Duplication and inconsistency: the same product has different codes or names in different branches.
  • Update anomalies: changing a supplier or product requires edits in several files; one missed edit creates conflicting facts.
  • Weak concurrent control: two staff members can overwrite each other's changes.
  • Security, recovery and reporting: permissions, audit trails, backups and consolidated reports are harder to manage consistently.

(b) DBMS comparison

A central or properly synchronized distributed DBMS would store common entities such as Product, Branch, Stock, Sale and Supplier, with primary and foreign keys enforcing consistent references.

Advantages (3) Disadvantages (2)
1. Less redundancy: one canonical product ID can be used by every branch. 1. Cost: software, migration, servers/cloud and administration cost money.
2. Integrity and concurrency: constraints and transactions reduce invalid or lost updates. 2. Complexity: design, training, maintenance and access management are needed.
3. Shared reporting/security/recovery: authorized users can query combined sales and stock; backup and auditing are centralized.

Recommendation: migrate to a relational DBMS, standardize product codes, import and deduplicate the spreadsheets, validate records, and give each branch role-based access. If branches need to operate during outages, use a planned offline/synchronization strategy rather than independent uncontrolled files.

File-based, DBMS and cloud-based approaches

Clinic data systems

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

(a) Current approach

The clinic has a manual/file-based hybrid (paper records plus local spreadsheets). Data is fragmented, searches require opening files or manually checking paper, and records can be duplicated or outdated. During an outbreak, this slows district-level reporting and may affect care.

(b) Compare and recommend

Approach Strengths Limitations for the clinic
Paper / local files Cheap to start; familiar; can work without internet. Slow search, duplicate records, weak sharing, fragile backups and limited audit/security.
DBMS on clinic server Structured records, SQL search, constraints, transactions, access control and backup. Clinic must manage server, maintenance, power, updates and remote access.
Cloud-hosted DBMS/application Authorized branches can access current data remotely; easier scaling and managed backup options. Internet dependency, subscription/vendor risk, privacy/legal requirements and configuration security.

Recommendation: use a secure clinic/hospital information system backed by a relational DBMS. A reputable cloud-hosted option is practical if connectivity, provider security, local regulations and budget are suitable; otherwise use a maintained local server with encrypted off-site backups and secure remote access.

Minimum design: Patient(PatientID, Name, DOB, District), Visit(VisitID, PatientID, VisitDate, Diagnosis, Status), and Admission(AdmissionID, VisitID, Ward, AdmitDate, DischargeDate). Index Patient.District and admission status/date so authorized staff can query admitted patients by district. Use unique patient identifiers, role-based permissions, audit logs and tested backups. Only authorized clinical staff should see sensitive details.

Google Forms + Sheets and DBMS

Data integrity & scaling

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

(a) Approach and limitations

Google Forms feeding Google Sheets is a cloud spreadsheet/file-based solution, not a full relational application designed for complex multi-user workflows. At 200 volunteers, duplicate submissions, manual overwrites, weak validation and ambiguous ownership of edits become more likely. Sheets can be useful at small scale, but sharing alone does not enforce all business rules.

(b) Why a DBMS is better

  • Constraints: primary keys and unique constraints can prevent the same relief case or recipient from being registered twice.
  • Referential integrity: a distribution record can refer only to an existing recipient, location and relief item.
  • Transactions: stock deduction and distribution recording can succeed together or roll back together.
  • Concurrent access: transactions and locking/isolation protect simultaneous updates.
  • Roles and audit: volunteers can enter data, supervisors approve it, and administrators review changes.
  • Queries and scale: reports can filter by district, date, recipient and item without merging many spreadsheets manually.
Recipient(RecipientID PK, NationalRef UNIQUE, Name, District)
ReliefItem(ItemID PK, ItemName, Unit)
Volunteer(VolunteerID PK, Name, Role)
Distribution(DistributionID PK, RecipientID FK, ItemID FK,
             VolunteerID FK, Qty, DistributedAt)

Import the existing sheets after deduplication, define what counts as a duplicate, validate required fields, and test the workflow with a pilot group. A DBMS will not magically remove bad data: validation rules, staff training and clear data ownership remain necessary.

Data independence

Logical & physical independence

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

(a) Which independence is violated?

The example mainly violates physical data independence: application programs depend on a hardcoded storage location/path. Moving the same logical data to another server should normally be an internal/storage change, not require rewriting every application. If the program also embeds file formats and record layouts, that coupling makes the problem worse.

(b) How DBMS independence helps

  • Physical data independence: changes to file organization, indexes, storage devices, partitions or server location are handled by the DBMS/storage configuration while the conceptual schema and user applications remain stable.
  • Logical data independence: certain changes to the conceptual schema (for example, adding an attribute or relation) can be hidden from unaffected external views and programs through mappings/views. It is generally harder to achieve fully than physical independence.

The DBA can move storage or rebuild indexes without changing those queries. If the server endpoint changes, configuration may need updating, but the data layout and application logic need not be rewritten.

Multi-user transaction processing

Concurrency control & ACID

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

Why the last seat can be sold twice

Without concurrency control, two counters may both read “1 seat remaining” before either update is saved. Each then sells the same seat. This is a race condition and violates the invariant that a seat may have at most one active booking.

ACID applied to ticket sales

  • Atomicity: booking creation and seat allocation are all-or-nothing. If payment/allocation fails, the transaction is rolled back or compensated according to the payment design.
  • Consistency: constraints preserve rules such as one active booking per trip/seat.
  • Isolation: concurrent transactions behave as if executed in a valid serial order; a second counter cannot successfully claim a seat already claimed by the first.
  • Durability: once committed, the booking survives a crash according to the DBMS durability guarantees.
BEGIN TRANSACTION
  Lock/check the selected trip-seat row
  If seat is still AVAILABLE:
      mark it BOOKED; insert booking
  Else:
      reject and ask customer to choose another seat
COMMIT (or ROLLBACK)

Use a database constraint such as UNIQUE(TripID, SeatNo) on active allocations (or a suitable partial unique index where supported), plus row-level locking or an atomic conditional update. An optimistic alternative is UPDATE TripSeat SET Status='BOOKED' WHERE TripID=? AND SeatNo=? AND Status='AVAILABLE'; accept only if exactly one row changed. A transaction should be short, and payment gateways require careful idempotency/reconciliation.

Efficiency: fine-grained locks allow unrelated seats/trips to be sold simultaneously, unlike locking the entire system. Correct isolation and constraints preserve the seat count without unnecessarily blocking all counters.

Bank account processing

Batch vs transaction processing

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

Problem

A month-end batch job locks all accounts for hours. This blocks independent ATM withdrawals and transfers, creating poor availability. A long global lock is broader than needed for most transactions.

Better transaction-processing design

  • Process each withdrawal, deposit or transfer as a short ACID transaction.
  • For a transfer, debit one account and credit the other in the same transaction; either both changes commit or neither does.
  • Use row-level locking, optimistic concurrency or appropriate isolation instead of locking every account.
  • Keep batch work incremental: aggregate eligible records, use snapshots/consistent reads, process in chunks, and schedule heavy jobs off-peak where needed.
  • Use indexes, monitoring, retries for deadlocks, transaction timeouts and idempotent job IDs to prevent duplicate processing.
BEGIN;
  SELECT balance FROM Account WHERE AccountID = A FOR UPDATE;
  Verify balance >= amount;
  UPDATE Account SET balance = balance - amount WHERE AccountID = A;
  UPDATE Account SET balance = balance + amount WHERE AccountID = B;
  INSERT INTO TransferLog(...);
COMMIT;

The example is conceptual SQL; production code must also validate account status, currency, limits and authorization. A batch report can read a consistent snapshot while live transactions continue, depending on DBMS isolation and workload. Reconciliation should confirm totals before a batch is marked complete.

Result: live users keep access, transactions remain consistent, and background processing can run in manageable units rather than freezing all accounts.

University database views

Multiple user views

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

A view is a named query that presents selected rows/columns from base tables. Different users can see different representations of the same underlying data without maintaining duplicate databases.

User Example view Benefit
Student StudentResultView(StudentID, CourseCode, Grade) filtered to the logged-in student Sees own courses and grades, not classmates' private records.
Faculty FacultyClassView(CourseID, StudentID, Attendance) for courses they teach Supports attendance and teaching tasks without access to finance or unrelated records.
Exam controller ExamOfficeView(StudentID, CourseID, Marks, Grade) Can verify results and prepare official reports.
DBA Catalog/monitoring views plus authorized administrative views Monitors schema, performance and access; privileges are still controlled separately. 
Base tables: Student, Course, Enrollment, Result
          |              |              |
   Student view     Faculty view   Exam-office view
     (own rows)    (assigned class) (authorized results)

Example SQL: CREATE VIEW PublishedResult AS SELECT StudentID, CourseID, Grade FROM Result WHERE IsPublished = TRUE; The application should additionally restrict the student to their own rows, for example with row-level security or a parameterized query; a generic view alone does not automatically identify the current user.

Important: views improve least-privilege access and simplicity, but security depends on grants, authentication, row filters, and preventing users from bypassing the application. A DBA has broad privileges only when needed and should be audited.

Hospital views

Views & privacy

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

(a) Receptionist privacy

Create a limited appointment/registration view that contains patient ID, name, appointment time and contact details needed for reception. Do not expose diagnosis, medical history, prescriptions or clinical notes.

(b) Billing staff

A billing view can combine the patient identifier, billable service, invoice amount, payment status and date. Staff can prepare invoices without opening the full medical record. Use only the clinical details necessary for billing.

(c) DBA

The DBA uses system catalog and monitoring views to manage indexes, users, permissions, backups and performance. Administrative access does not mean routine unrestricted browsing of patient records; use least privilege and audit access.

Patient ──< Appointment ──> ClinicalRecord
   |              |
   +---------- Billing / Invoice

ReceptionView: PatientID, Name, AppointmentTime
BillingView: PatientID, InvoiceID, Amount, PaymentStatus
ClinicalView: authorized clinicians only

Illustrative SQL:

CREATE VIEW ReceptionAppointments AS
SELECT p.PatientID, p.Name, a.AppointmentTime
FROM Patient p JOIN Appointment a ON a.PatientID = p.PatientID;

Grant reception access to this view, not the underlying clinical tables. Apply row-level restrictions where appropriate, use strong authentication and encryption, keep audit logs, and periodically review permissions. Views reduce accidental exposure but must be combined with proper database grants and operational controls.

Garments factory user groups

External views

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

In the three-schema model, an external schema is a user/group-specific view of the database. The conceptual schema describes the whole logical database, while each external view shows only what a particular role needs.

Group External view example Appropriate granularity
Floor supervisors ProductionLineView(LineID, Shift, TargetQty, ProducedQty, DefectCount) Operational detail by line/shift; no salary or personal HR data.
HR EmployeeHRView(EmployeeID, Name, Department, LeaveBalance) Employee and staffing data; salary/disciplinary fields only for specifically authorized HR roles.
Top management ExecutiveSummaryView(Department, Month, Output, DefectRate, CostTotal) Aggregated monthly KPIs, not every worker's row. 
Floor supervisor ──> ProductionLineView ──┐
HR              ──> EmployeeHRView ────────┼──> Conceptual schema
Executives      ──> ExecutiveSummaryView ──┘

Security: expose only required columns and rows, enforce role grants and audit access. Simplicity: users see business terms and useful summaries rather than complex joins. Independence: a view can often remain stable when internal storage changes; mappings translate it to the conceptual schema. Views do not replace authentication or careful authorization.

 Mobile app / Customer support / Compliance

                       |
                CONCEPTUAL LEVEL
   User(UserID, Name) · Account(AccountID, UserID, Balance)

Purpose of each level

  • External: each application sees a task-specific view. Example: a customer sees their account number (masked), available balance and own transactions, but not another customer's records or internal fraud flags.
  • Conceptual: the complete logical structure and relationships, independent of the precise physical storage. Four example entities are User, Account, Transaction and BillPayment.
  • Internal: how data is physically represented: indexes, pages, partitions, log files, compression and storage allocation.

Logical data independence is between external and conceptual levels: an internal conceptual change can be hidden from unaffected external views. Physical data independence is between conceptual and internal levels: storage/index changes need not alter the logical schema or application views.

Add insurance report and migrate DBMS

Mappings & change management

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

Adding an insurance report

Create a new external schema/view for insurance staff, built from the existing conceptual entities such as Patient, Visit, Invoice and InsuranceClaim. Grant only necessary fields. Existing external views continue to work because the new report is an additional view, not a replacement of the conceptual schema.

Migrating storage/DBMS

Map the same conceptual tables and views to the new DBMS's internal representation. Recreate tables, constraints, indexes, stored routines and permissions; convert data types; migrate and validate data; then switch the connection/configuration after testing. Applications can remain unchanged if the SQL/API behavior and schema compatibility are preserved.

Existing app ──> Existing external view ──┐
Insurance app ─> Insurance report view ───┼─> Conceptual schema
                                          |
                               mapping / adapter
                                          |
                        DBMS A storage  →  DBMS B storage

Mappings are the translation rules between levels. The external–conceptual mapping connects a view to logical data; the conceptual–internal mapping translates logical relations into the chosen storage structures. The latter enables physical independence. The new insurance view demonstrates external-level flexibility and can demonstrate logical independence if it is isolated from existing views.

Qualification: mappings do not guarantee zero effort for every migration. Vendor-specific SQL, transaction semantics, data types, triggers, security behavior and performance may differ. A compatibility layer, migration tests, reconciliation, backup and rollback plan are needed.

External level: passenger sees their own booking and seat; counter staff see the trips and seats they are authorized to sell; administrators see operational reports.

Internal level: specifies indexes, files, partitions, logging and storage details.

  • Logical data independence: between external and conceptual levels. For example, adding a new conceptual attribute such as passenger email should not affect the passenger's existing booking view.
  • Physical data independence: between conceptual and internal levels. Rebuilding indexes or partitioning trips by date should not require changes to the logical booking schema or applications.

Relational schema, keys and cardinalities

Library database

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

Relational schema

MEMBER(
  MemberID PK, FullName, Phone, Email
)
BOOK(
  BookID PK, ISBN UNIQUE, Title, Author, Publisher
)
BOOK_COPY(
  CopyID PK, BookID FK → BOOK.BookID,
  Branch, Shelf, CopyStatus
)
LOAN(
  LoanID PK, CopyID FK → BOOK_COPY.CopyID,
  MemberID FK → MEMBER.MemberID,
  LoanDate, DueDate, ReturnDate NULL
)
FINE(
  FineID PK, LoanID FK → LOAN.LoanID UNIQUE,
  Amount, AssessedAt, PaidAt NULL, FineStatus
)

Cardinalities

BOOK 1 ─────< BOOK_COPY
MEMBER 1 ───< LOAN >──── 1 BOOK_COPY
LOAN 1 ───── 0..1 FINE
  • One book title can have many physical copies; each copy belongs to one title.
  • One member can have many loans over time; each loan is for one member and one copy.
  • A copy can appear in many loans over time, but business rules must prevent overlapping active loans for the same copy.
  • A loan has zero or one fine under the stated “fine per overdue loan” rule; the unique FK in FINE enforces at most one fine per loan.

Why separate BOOK and BOOK_COPY? A title can exist in multiple physical copies, each with its own status/location and loan history. A composite key such as (BookID, CopyNo) is also possible instead of CopyID. Use foreign keys, non-null due dates and checks such as DueDate >= LoanDate. Whether an overdue fine exists can be calculated from due/return dates; if fines are stored, define a policy for updating them.

University registration

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

Relational schema

STUDENT(StudentID PK, Name, Email UNIQUE, Program)
COURSE(CourseID PK, CourseTitle, Credits)
INSTRUCTOR(InstructorID PK, Name, Email UNIQUE)
SECTION(SectionID PK, CourseID FK, InstructorID FK,
        Term, SectionNo, Room, Schedule,
        UNIQUE(CourseID, Term, SectionNo))

Cardinalities

COURSE 1 ─────< SECTION >───── 1 INSTRUCTOR

A course may run in many sections; each section belongs to one course. Each section is taught by one instructor, while one instructor may teach many sections. Student-to-section enrollment is many-to-many and is resolved by ENROLLMENT.

The composite primary key (StudentID, SectionID) prevents a student from enrolling twice in the same section. Foreign keys prevent enrollment into nonexistent students or sections. Additional rules can limit credits per term, prevent timetable conflicts and restrict enrollment when a section is full; these often require application logic or carefully designed constraints/transactions.

PATIENT 1 ───< APPOINTMENT >─── 1 DOCTOR

Mobile banking database

GRAPHICAL DIAGRAM

Relational schema

APP_USER(UserID PK, FullName, Phone UNIQUE, KYCStatus)
AGENT(AgentID PK, Name, AgentCode UNIQUE, Status)
ACCOUNT(AccountID PK, UserID FK, AccountType, Currency,
        Balance, Status)
TRANSACTION(TxnID PK, AccountID FK, AgentID FK NULL,
            TxnType, Amount, Currency, CreatedAt,
            Reference UNIQUE, Status)
BILL_PAYMENT(BillPaymentID PK, TxnID FK UNIQUE,
             BillerCode, CustomerRef, BillPeriod)

Cardinalities

APP_USER 1 ─────< ACCOUNT
ACCOUNT 1 ──────< TRANSACTION >──── 0..1 AGENT
TRANSACTION 1 ── 0..1 BILL_PAYMENT

A user can own multiple accounts; each account belongs to one user in this simple model. An agent can process many transactions; agent is nullable for self-service transactions. BILL_PAYMENT is an optional subtype/detail associated with a transaction. If a transaction can contain both debit and credit accounts (e.g., transfer), use a TRANSACTION table plus TRANSACTION_ENTRY(TxnID, AccountID, Direction, Amount) rather than only one AccountID.

Important financial design: production systems should not rely only on overwriting a balance. Record immutable ledger entries, enforce idempotency using a unique reference, and update account balances atomically inside a transaction. Enforce positive amount, valid status transitions, currency consistency, authentication and audit requirements.

Entities, composite/multivalued attributes and weak entity

Coaching center ER design

CHEN ER DIAGRAM · ENTITY / ATTRIBUTE / RELATIONSHIP

ER diagram

TEACHER M ─────< TEACHES >───── M BATCH
                                  |
STUDENT N ─── belongs to ─── 1 BATCH

STUDENT M ─────< EXAM_RESULT >───── M EXAM
                    (Marks)

EXAM 1 ═══════< QUESTION
             weak entity
       partial key: QuestionNo
       PK = (ExamID, QuestionNo)

Entities and attributes

  • STUDENT(StudentID, Name, Phone, Address{House, Road, City}, BatchID FK). Address is composite. If a student may have multiple phone numbers, model StudentPhone(StudentID, Phone) as a separate multivalued attribute/entity.
  • BATCH(BatchID, Name, Schedule).
  • TEACHER(TeacherID, Name, Phone, Email).
  • TEACHES(TeacherID FK, BatchID FK, composite PK). A teacher can teach many batches; each batch has at least one teacher, so Batch participation in TEACHES is mandatory.
  • EXAM(ExamID, Title, ExamDate).
  • EXAM_RESULT(StudentID FK, ExamID FK, Marks, composite PK). This resolves the M:N student-exam relationship and records marks per student per exam.
  • QUESTION is a weak entity owned by EXAM. Its partial key is QuestionNo; full PK is (ExamID, QuestionNo). Attributes: QuestionText, Marks, AnswerKey. It cannot exist without its exam.

Cardinalities and constraints

  • Each student belongs to exactly one batch (Student N:1 Batch); each batch may contain many students.
  • Teacher–Batch is M:N; each batch has one or more teachers.
  • Student–Exam is M:N; each result records one student's marks in one exam.
  • Exam–Question is 1:N identifying relationship; each exam has multiple questions, and a question's identity is scoped to its exam.

For implementation, use foreign keys and composite keys as described. Enforce “at least one teacher per batch” and “each exam has multiple questions” through a transaction/business rule or deferred validation; ordinary foreign keys do not enforce minimum child counts.

Composite key and booking-service relationship

Hotel chain ER design

CHEN ER DIAGRAM · ENTITY / ATTRIBUTE / RELATIONSHIP

ER diagram

HOTEL 1 ─────< ROOM
PK HotelID       PK (HotelID, RoomNo)
                     |
                     | 1
                     |
                     N
GUEST 1 ─────< BOOKING >───── 1 ROOM
                PK BookingID
                CheckIn, CheckOut

BOOKING M ─────< BOOKING_SERVICE >───── M SERVICE
                 Quantity, Charge

Entities and attributes

  • HOTEL(HotelID, Name, City).
  • ROOM(HotelID FK, RoomNo, Type, Rate). The room number is only unique within a hotel, so use composite PK (HotelID, RoomNo).
  • GUEST(GuestID, Name, Phone, Address{Street, City}). Address is composite.
  • BOOKING(BookingID, GuestID FK, HotelID, RoomNo, CheckIn, CheckOut). Use composite FK (HotelID, RoomNo) to ROOM. Each booking is for exactly one room; each room may be referenced by many bookings over time.
  • SERVICE(ServiceID, Name, UnitCharge).
  • BOOKING_SERVICE(BookingID FK, ServiceID FK, Quantity, Charge, composite PK (BookingID, ServiceID) or a line ID if a service can appear on multiple separate lines).

Cardinalities and business rules

  • Hotel 1:N Room; a room belongs to exactly one hotel.
  • Guest 1:N Booking; each booking belongs to exactly one guest.
  • Room 1:N Booking across time; dates for the same room must not overlap for active bookings.
  • Booking M:N Service via BOOKING_SERVICE. Quantity and actual Charge belong to this relationship because the amount charged can differ from today's service rate.

Validate CheckOut > CheckIn, positive quantity and nonnegative charge. A room availability check must be transactional to prevent two users from booking overlapping dates concurrently. Save the charge at time of service for invoice history.

  • PLAYER_MATCH(PlayerID FK, MatchID FK, RunsScored, WicketsTaken, composite PK (PlayerID, MatchID)). This resolves the M:N participation and stores each player's statistics for that match.
  • INNINGS is a weak entity owned by MATCH: PK (MatchID, InningsNo), with Runs and Overs. InningsNo is unique only within its match.

Cardinalities and constraints

  • Each player belongs to exactly one team; a team has many players.
  • A coach may coach many teams, but each team has exactly one head coach. The FK in TEAM expresses the one-coach-per-team rule; a coach may be referenced by multiple teams.
  • Player–Match is M:N, resolved through PLAYER_MATCH.
  • Match–Innings is identifying 1:N; an innings cannot exist without its match.

Use checks such as nonnegative runs/wickets and a valid overs range appropriate to the match format. “Each match has multiple innings” is a minimum-participation rule that needs application/deferred validation beyond a basic FK. The innings composite PK correctly scopes the innings number to its match.
